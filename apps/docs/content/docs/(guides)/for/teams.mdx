A team needs one draft that everyone is looking at, one world that every title agrees with, and an answer to "is this done?" that everyone can see. In Plotra the workspace holds the people and the projects. Members write in the same note at the same time, with cursors, comments and suggestions. One project can be the world that the others borrow their characters and places from. Stages of your own say who must approve a note and lock it when it is finished, and a changelog shows what changed since a release.

## Set up

1. **Make a workspace for the team.** Whoever creates it is its owner. Keep it apart from your personal workspace: every member of a workspace can open and edit every project in it. See [Workspaces](/docs/settings/account#workspaces).
2. **Invite the room.** Under **Settings → Members & invites**, enter an address, pick **Member** or **Admin** and press **Send invite**. An invitation is good for 48 hours. See [Inviting people to the workspace](/docs/collaboration/sharing#inviting-people-to-the-workspace).
3. **Decide who manages.** Only the owner and admins can change a project's tools and workflow, share it, add review rules and cut a release. Make the leads admins.
4. **Bring in outsiders by project.** A freelance editor, a consultant or a beta reader is given one project by email, as **Editor**, **Commenter** or **Viewer**, and sees nothing else. See [Sharing a project](/docs/collaboration/sharing#sharing-a-project).
5. **Set up the world.** Create one project of the type **Franchise / IP bible**, which starts with **World bible** on. In each title's project, open **Project settings → General** and choose it under **Shared world bible**.
6. **Make a template.** Switch on **Offer as a template** (Project settings → General) on a project that has your folders, note types, workflow and labels. New projects can then start in its shape. See [Templates](/docs/settings/project#templates).

| Role | In a project | In the workspace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Owner** | Everything, including settings and sharing | Members, the AI switch, the plan and billing |
| **Admin** | Everything, including settings and sharing | Members |
| **Member** | Read, comment and edit | Creates projects |
| **Editor**, **Commenter**, **Viewer** (guests) | Their role, in that project only | Nothing |

## Typical workflow

1. **Build the bible.** In the world project, give characters, places and factions their pages and mark what is **Canon**. See [The world bible](/docs/canon#the-world-bible).
2. **Start each title as its own project.** A book, a season and a game each get a project of their own type, pointed at the shared world. Its pages appear in their Codex and link picker, read-only. See [Shared worlds](/docs/writing/links#shared-worlds).
3. **Choose a workflow.** Under **Project settings → Workflow**, pick the preset **Draft → In review → Approved → Locked** or build your own stages. Tick **Approval** on a stage that needs sign-off and **Locked** on one that is finished. See [Workflow](/docs/settings/project#workflow).
4. **Name the reviewers.** Switch on **Review & editorial**, open **Reviews → Rules** and add a rule: a person must approve everything in a folder, everything with a tag, or the whole project. See [Required reviewers and review rules](/docs/modules/editorial#required-reviewers-and-review-rules).
5. **Write together.** Open the same note and type; text is merged as you go. Talk in comments, with `@` to mention a member, and use suggesting mode for changes someone else should decide. See [Writing at the same time](/docs/collaboration#writing-at-the-same-time).
6. **Ask for review.** In the note's **Review** section, choose the stage and press **Request review**. Each reviewer is notified and finds the note under **Waiting for you**. When the last one approves, the note moves to the stage by itself. See [Requesting a review](/docs/modules/editorial#requesting-a-review).
7. **Lock what is finished.** A note in a locked stage can be read and commented on, but changed only by its approvers: its required reviewers and the people who manage the project. See [Locked content](/docs/modules/editorial#locked-content).
8. **Cut a release.** With **Releases & reveals** on, add the release in the roadmap. Then **Reviews → Changelog → Cut release** keeps a snapshot of every note. Later the changelog lists what was added, changed, removed and retconned since. See [The changelog per release](/docs/modules/editorial#the-changelog-per-release).
9. **Keep up.** The bell lists mentions, replies, review requests and decisions; the activity feed lists what changed in the project. Each person chooses a daily digest, an email for each one, or none. See [Notifications](/docs/collaboration/notifications).

## Sizing the plan

A plan belongs to the workspace, not to a person, and nobody pays per seat.

| | Free | Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $0 | $10 a month, or $96 a year, for the whole workspace |
| Workspace members, counting the owner and open invitations | 3 | 25 |
| People one project can be shared with | 5 | 200 |
| Projects | 10 | Unlimited |
| Storage for files and versions | 50 MB | 50 GB |
| Hosted AI | None | 1,500 credits a month, shared by the workspace |

Up to three people fit on Free. A room of four or more needs Pro, and so does a workspace whose world project and titles add up to more than 10 projects. A team that needs more than 25 members can arrange an Enterprise plan. See [Plans and billing](/docs/settings/plans).

## Things to know

- **A member can edit every project.** A workspace role can't be narrowed for one project. To keep someone out of a project, don't make them a member: share the projects they should see one by one.
- **There are no permissions on single notes or folders.** Everything in a project follows the project.
- **Reviewers and mentions go by the member list.** A guest can't be named a required reviewer or mentioned with `@`, whatever their project role.
- **Locks work without the module.** A locked stage is enforced in every project. Review & editorial is what lets you name reviewers and record decisions.
- **A shared world goes one level deep** and must be in the same workspace. Only characters, locations and world entries are shared, not releases. Links into the world show in the graph and on the entry's page under **Mentioned here**, but not in the world project's own **Mentioned in** lists. The spoiler horizon doesn't apply to shared pages.
- **Someone who can't open the world project** sees its links as "Restricted note".
- **Cutting a release needs someone who manages the project**, and its snapshots count toward the workspace's storage.
- **Word counts are for the project, not for each person.** The Stats view's daily total includes everyone's words.
- **Nobody can leave a workspace or change their own role.** An owner or admin removes a member. A project can't be archived, but an owner or admin can [delete it](/docs/settings/project#deleting-a-project).
- **Open invitations use a seat** until they are accepted, cancelled or expire.
