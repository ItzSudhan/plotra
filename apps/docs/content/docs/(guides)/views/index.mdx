title: Views at a glance
description: See the same story as a timeline, a corkboard, a plot grid or a storyboard, and what each view is good for.
---

When the prose stops telling you what is wrong, look at the story another way. A view is a way of looking at your project. Views open as tabs, so you can put a timeline next to the scene you are writing. They all read the same notes: change something in one and the others follow.

![A scene and the corkboard of its chapter open side by side as two panes](/shots/panes-split.webp)

There are three ways to open a view:

- **The ribbon** on the left. It starts with the five views your kind of project uses most (see [Project types](/docs/reference/project-types#the-ribbon-a-project-starts-with)). Every other view is under **More**, grouped as Plan, World, Read and publish, Team and progress, and Tools, each with a line saying what it is for. You choose which ones stay in the ribbon, and in what order: see [The ribbon](/docs/settings/customize#the-ribbon). A few buttons use a shorter name than the view: **Relations** for Relationships, **Releases** for the Release roadmap and **Secrets** for Secrets & setups.
- **All commands** (`⌘P`). Each view has a command in the **Views** group; the names are in the tables below.
- **The binder.** Right-click a folder or a chapter to open it in a view that works on a folder. A timeline, a canvas or a dialogue tree opens when you click it, like any note.

## Views that are always there

| View | What it shows | Good for | Command |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Editor** | One note. | Writing. See [The editor](/docs/writing/editor). | Click a note |
| **Graph** | Notes as dots, links as lines, for the whole project or around one note. | Seeing what connects to what, and what connects to nothing. | "Open graph view" (`⌘G`) |
| **Codex** | Characters, places and other world pages, grouped by type, like a wiki. | Looking things up. | "Open codex" (`⌘⌥⇧C`) |
| **Relationships** | Characters, with labelled lines between them: ally, rival, parent. | Keeping who-is-what-to-whom straight. | "Open relationship map" (`⌘⌥⇧R`) |
| **Outline** | A table of every note with its properties as columns. Edit in place, sort and filter. | Checking status, point of view and word counts across a book. | "Open outline" |
| **Corkboard** | A folder's scenes as index cards with title and synopsis. | Reordering. Drag a card and the binder order changes with it. | "Open corkboard of current folder" |
| **Plot grid** | Scenes down the side, plot threads across the top. | Seeing which thread goes quiet for too long. | "Open plot grid" |
| **Manuscript** | A folder's scenes stitched into one long, editable page. | Reading and revising across scene breaks. | "Read the whole draft (manuscript of current folder)" |
| **Read** | A paginated, print-style preview of a folder. | Proofreading, and printing to PDF through your browser. | "Read and print preview of current folder" |
| **Activity** | What changed in the project, and who changed it. | Catching up. See [Notifications](/docs/collaboration/notifications). | "Open activity feed" |
| **Stats** | Words per day, the streak, and progress towards your goals. | Keeping the draft moving. See [Writing goals](/docs/writing/goals). | "Open writing stats and goals" |

### Views that work on a folder

Corkboard, Plot grid, Manuscript and Read work on a folder or a chapter. So do Production, Translate and Publish when their modules are on. There are two ways to say which folder:

- Right-click a folder or a chapter in the binder and choose the view. A note of any other type offers the same views once it has notes inside it.
- Open the view from the ribbon or **All commands**. It opens on the folder or chapter you have selected or open. If the note you have open has nothing inside it, the view opens on the folder that holds it. With nothing selected, it covers the whole project.

## Views that are notes

A **timeline**, a **canvas** and a **map** are notes in the binder. Create one like any other note and click it to open it. A project can have several of each. "New timeline", "New canvas" and "New map" in **All commands** create one. Timelines and canvases are covered in full in [Timeline and canvas](/docs/views/timeline-and-canvas), maps in [Maps](/docs/views/maps).

### Timeline

Story time on a horizontal axis. Scenes sit where their **Story date** property says and last as long as their **Duration**; drag a scene and the properties change. You can add events that are not scenes, split the timeline into lanes by plot thread, point of view or location, zoom from minutes to centuries, switch between story order and reading order with flashbacks marked, and use a calendar of your own invention.

Everything is in [Timeline and canvas](/docs/views/timeline-and-canvas#timeline).

### Canvas (storyboard)

An endless board for arranging things by hand: cards that show real notes, plain text cards, image and shot panels, groups, and labelled arrows between them. Several people can work on one canvas at once. A note placed on a canvas counts as a link to it.

Everything is in [Timeline and canvas](/docs/views/timeline-and-canvas#canvas).

### Map

A picture of your world (a drawn map, a floor plan, a star chart) with pins, regions and paths on it, each standing for a note, in layers you can show and hide. A pin can stand for another map, so a continent's map opens a city's.

Everything is in [Maps](/docs/views/maps).

### Dialogue

A **dialogue** note is a branching conversation. It can be created only while the Interactive narrative module is on. See [Interactive narrative](/docs/modules/interactive).

## Views that come with a module

A project's **modules** switch groups of tools on and off, so a novelist doesn't see game tools. The project type picks the starting set. Change it in [Project settings](/docs/settings/project). A view whose module is off is not in the ribbon, under **More**, or in **All commands**.

| Module | Views it adds | Where it is covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World bible | Continuity | [Canon](/docs/canon/secrets-and-continuity#continuity-checks) |
| Releases & reveals | Release roadmap, Secrets & setups | [Canon](/docs/canon/releases-and-reveals#the-release-roadmap-view) |
| Research & sources | Sources | [Research](/docs/modules/research) |
| Review & editorial | Reviews | [Editorial](/docs/modules/editorial) |
| Production | Production | [Production](/docs/modules/production) |
| Interactive narrative | Dialogue, Quests, Strings | [Interactive narrative](/docs/modules/interactive) |
| Localization | Translate | [Localization](/docs/modules/localization) |
| Publishing | Publish | [Publishing](/docs/modules/publishing) |
| AI agent | AI | [AI](/docs/ai) |

The commands are "Open release roadmap", "Open secrets & setups", "Open continuity", "Open sources", "Open reviews", "Open production", "Open quest designer", "Open strings (short game text)", "Open translate", "Open publish" and "Open AI chat". The AI view is offered only while AI is on for the workspace, the project and you.

A [plugin](/docs/developers/plugins#a-panel) of yours can add a panel that opens in a pane like a view. It opens from its own command in **All commands**, never from the ribbon.

## Renaming a view

A project can give any view a name of its own: the **Codex** can be the "Wiki", the **Release roadmap** the "Campaign". The new name is used in the ribbon, the **More** menu, the view's tabs, a folder's right-click menu and **All commands** ("Open Wiki"), for everyone in the project. The words inside the view stay the app's. See [Views and tools](/docs/settings/project#views-and-tools).

## Views and the spoiler horizon

When a [spoiler horizon](/docs/canon/releases-and-reveals#the-spoiler-horizon) is on, the Outline, Graph, Codex, Timeline, Read and Quests views leave out what the audience has not been told yet, and a map doesn't draw the pins, regions and paths for it. The Codex filters this project's pages, not those of a shared world. The Corkboard, Plot grid, Manuscript, Relationships and Canvas views show everything regardless, and so do search and **Find a note**. The full list is in [What it hides](/docs/canon/releases-and-reveals#what-it-hides).

## Where a view keeps its settings

Some choices are kept with your account. They follow you to every project and every computer, and your co-writers make their own:

| View | What it remembers |
| --- | --- |
| Outline | The property columns you chose, and the sort. |
| Corkboard | The card size, what cards show, and the fields on them. |
| Plot grid | **Chapters as rows**, **Each scene's synopsis**, and the fields shown as columns. |
| Graph | The note types you hid, **Steps away**, **Unlinked notes**, **Links from fields**, and **Graph** or **List**. |
| Relationships | What the map shows, **Map** or **Family tree**, and **Only related**. |
| Read | The paper size, US Letter or A4, set in [Customize](/docs/settings/customize#what-you-can-change). |

Some things are kept in your browser. They won't follow you to another computer: the positions on the relationship map, the zoom and position of each timeline, which continuity warnings you dismissed, the spoiler horizon, and the pane layout you didn't save as a named layout. Your writing session's word count is kept for that tab only.

A timeline's calendar, lanes, mode and reading-order line are stored on the timeline itself, so everyone sees the same. A canvas and a map are shared documents: every card, mark and connection is the same for everyone. Plot threads and relationships belong to the project.

Some things are not kept at all and reset when you close the tab: the filters in the outline, the graph's tag list and search, the codex's search box, the layers you hid on a map, and the graph's positions.
