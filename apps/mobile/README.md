# Plotra for phones

Plotra on iPhone and Android, built with Expo (SDK 58) and Expo Router. It talks to Plotra Cloud, or a self-hosted Plotra, through the same API as everything else (`apps/app/app/api/v1`), with a token of its own.

## What it does

- **Library**: every project, by workspace, and the notes you opened lately.
- **A project's binder**: folders, chapters, scenes and notes in the web app's order. Open and close folders, filter by title or tag, hold a note for its menu. Move a note up or down, or "Move to…" a folder or chapter (at its end, its start, or after a note in it), or use **Arrange notes**: tap a note to pick it up, tap where it goes, or nudge it with the arrows. Duplicate a note and what's inside it.
- **Trash**: what's been moved there, when and by whom. Put a note back, delete it forever, or empty the trash, with the web's permissions.
- **Reading**: a note drawn from its own document with all its formatting, as the web's editor draws it (script pages, callouts, tables, to-do lists, highlights, suggestions marked), by a read-only Plate in a web view (`src/components/rich-editor/note-reader.tsx`, through `src/components/note-text.tsx`) that grows to fit inside the screen's scrolling. The document comes from `/api/mobile/note-value` and is kept for offline; while the reader starts, or if the document isn't on the phone, the Markdown stands in. Text is selectable, `[[links]]` open the note they name, web links open in the browser, and Previous / Next read straight through. Boards and dialogues open in their own screens (below); timelines on the web.
- **History**: a note's versions (its menu → History): the named ones and the automatic ones kept every half hour. Read one with the same reader, save what the note says now as a version, rename or delete one, and restore one: the note goes back to it for everyone, through its live document, and what it said is kept as a version first, as on the web. Through `/api/mobile/history` (`src/lib/reader-api.ts`), which runs the web's own version functions.
- **Writing**: the note's own document, live, in Plate (the web's editor) running in a web view (`src/components/rich-editor`, an Expo DOM component) joined to the note's room on the collab server. There's nothing to save: every keystroke goes into the shared document, people writing elsewhere appear as they type, and comments, suggestions, block tags and formatting the phone doesn't show stay as they are. A formatting bar sits above the keyboard (bold, italic, headings, lists, to-dos, quotes, scene breaks, undo; script elements in a script), and `[[` opens a note picker to link; tapping a link while writing offers to open it. Other people's cursors and selections show with their names and colours. People who may edit can switch to suggesting, and accept or reject suggestions one at a time (tap one) or all at once; the people who made them are told (`POST /api/mobile/suggestions`). Passages with open comments are highlighted (tap one for its thread), and selected text can be commented on, by commenters too. Pictures come from the photo library or the camera, with an optional description, and are stored like the web's, within the workspace's storage (`/api/mobile/upload`). The document is kept on the phone, so you can write offline; what you write then goes when you're back, from the editor or, if it's closed, from the outbox (`POST /api/mobile/doc`).
- **Details**: title, synopsis, tags, other names and the project's statuses (these wait on the phone when offline), and, online, the note's custom fields (text, numbers, dates, choices, yes or no, web links, links to notes), its canon status, when the audience learns it (release, where, truth or belief), its label and its world page type. New notes of every kind the project makes, in its own words: scenes, chapters, research, boards, timelines, dialogues and world pages of each of its types, made as the web's **+ New** makes them (`src/lib/binder-api.ts`, `apps/app/lib/api/binder.ts`).
- **Quick note**: catch an idea as a new note in any project, or at the end of a note you pick.
- **Search** across all your projects at once, or one. Every project's text is kept on the phone, so search finds words inside notes offline too. It's fetched again after you change a note, when you come back to the app (at most every half hour per project), and when you pull down in Search or tap "Keep projects searchable offline" in Account.
- **As of a release**: pick a book, episode or session in a project, and the binder dims what readers don't know yet, the reader leaves out passages revealed later, and the editor dims them.
- **Comments**: a note's comment threads (its menu → Comments, with how many are open), open ones first and resolved ones folded away. Reply, resolve or reopen, comment on the whole note, and hold a comment of yours to edit or delete it (Undo brings it back). `@` suggests the project's people, who are told. A reply written offline waits in the outbox; other changes say you're offline. Through `/api/mobile/comments` (`src/lib/comments-api.ts`), which runs the web's own comment functions.
- **Story views** (a project's menu → Timeline, Corkboard, Plot grid, Codex), through `/api/mobile/views` (`src/lib/views-api.ts`, laid out by `src/lib/views-model.ts`), which runs the web's own story functions. Each is kept for offline and follows the project's "as of" release as the web does: the timeline and codex show only what readers know by then, the corkboard and plot grid dim the rest, as the binder does.
  - **Timeline**: story time read top to bottom, a heading per day, in the timeline's own calendar (`src/lib/story-calendar.ts` is a copy of the web's). Scenes come from their Story date and Duration, events are the timeline's own. Lanes (plot threads, POV, location) are chips to filter by. Add, change and delete events; give a scene a Story date, or change it. Tap a scene to read it.
  - **Corkboard**: a folder's or chapter's children as index cards, with synopsis, status, word count and colour label. Tap to read (a folder opens its own cards), hold for more. Reorder with Arrange (arrows on each card) or the card's menu, which reorders the binder too. New scene and chapter cards.
  - **Plot grid**: follow one thread through the scenes, or go scene by scene and see every thread in it. Tap a cell to write what happens, add a scene to a thread or take it out. Add, rename, reopen and delete threads.
  - **Codex**: characters, places, research, notes and the project's world-bible types, with counts, find by name or alias, and each entry's page: canon status, fields, its text and what mentions it. Simple fields (text, number, date, link, choice, yes/no) can be changed; new characters, places and notes.
- **Chat**: the project's chat (its menu → Chat) and each note's (the note's menu → Chat about this note), newest at the bottom, older ones as you scroll up. `@` suggests the project's people, who are told; hold a message to copy it, or delete your own (or anyone's, if you manage the project). New messages arrive at once: the app listens on the project's presence room on the collab server, as the web does, and announces what it sends there (`src/lib/presence.ts`); it also looks again every so often while open, and when you come back. Through `/api/mobile/chat` (`src/lib/chat-api.ts`), which runs the web's own chat functions.
- **Ask AI** (a project's menu, or a note's → Ask AI about this note), where AI is on for you: the web panel's agent, run on the server by the same code (`apps/app/lib/agent/answer.ts`), on your own model, in Ask, Suggest or Auto. The answer appears as it's written (read with `expo/fetch`, which streams), with each step it took, the notes it used, the changes it wants your yes for, and what it changed, with Undo run. What it writes goes into the live documents, as on the web. `@` points it at a note. Your chats are kept on the server and listed under the clock button. Where AI is off, the screen says why and who turns it on (a workspace owner, on the web); a model on your own computer can't be reached from the phone. "Ask AI" isn't offered at all where a workspace promised no AI. Through `/api/mobile/agent` (`src/lib/agent-api.ts`).
- **Graph and relationships** (a project's menu → Graph, Relationships; a note's menu → Graph around this note), drawn by one SVG canvas in a web view (`src/components/maps/map-canvas.tsx`, an Expo DOM component): drag to pan, pinch to zoom, tap to pick. The graph shows the project's notes and links, or those one to three steps around a note, laid out on the phone (`src/components/maps/layout.ts`, a small force layout, since graphology isn't in the app), filtered by kind, with or without unlinked notes, and as a list. The relationship map shows characters or places, on a circle, as the web's family tree (from "Parent of", "Married to", "Sibling of"), or as a list; tap a line or a person's relationship to change it, add new ones with the web's types. Both follow the project's "as of" release: notes and people readers don't know yet are left out. Through `/api/mobile/maps/links` and `/api/mobile/maps/relationships` (`src/lib/maps-api.ts`), which run the web's own functions.
- **Boards** (opening a board from the binder or the reader): its cards, groups and arrows, read live from the board's room (`/api/mobile/maps/board`), with a list as well. Tap a note card to open its note. Someone who can edit the board can move cards (More → Move cards, then drag), add a text card where they tap, and change a text card or sticky note's text; each change goes into the board's live document, so anyone with it open on the web sees it arrive. Boards made as maps list what's pinned on them.
- **Dialogues**: play a branching dialogue through as a player would (choices, conditions and variables worked out by a copy of the web's play-through, `src/components/maps/dialogue`), step back, start again, see the variables; or read every card in an outline with where each one leads. Read from the dialogue's room (`/api/mobile/maps/dialogue`).
- **Inbox**: mentions, replies, shares and reviews, with a badge on the tab. A mention or reply opens the comments at its thread; a chat mention opens that chat.
- **Notifications on the phone**: the app asks only when you say so (the "Get notified on this phone" card in Inbox, or the switch in Account). A push says what the bell says, tapping it opens the note, thread or chat, and the app icon's badge is the unread count. The switch turns them off for all your phones; signing out (or revoking the app's token in Settings) removes the phone on the server. See `src/lib/push.ts` and `apps/app/lib/push.ts`.
- **Offline**: everything you've opened is saved on the phone and shown when there's no connection. Changes made offline wait on the phone and are sent when you're back (Account lists them, and keeps the text of any the server turns down).
- **Account**: light, dark or the phone's setting; reading text size; links to the web; sign out.
- **Sharing**: share a project by email as a viewer, commenter or editor; change or take away someone's role (with Undo); a workspace's members (Account → Your workspaces): owners and admins invite people, resend or cancel invitations, change roles and remove people, and anyone can leave. Those go through the web's own functions, with a two-minute sign-in session the server makes for the phone's token (apps/app/lib/api/auth-session.ts), so the web's rules and the invitation email apply; the read-only links: on or off, copy or send, how far readers can see, a new address, another link, remove one.
- **Project settings**: title, logline, the kind of story, the format new scenes are written in, the tools on and off, the shared world bible, automatic versions, offering it as a template, and deleting it (its title typed back). The workflow, words, labels, kinds, webhooks and GitHub open on the web.
- **Your settings**: your name, your photo (picked, then cut to a small square on the phone, as the web does: `src/components/photo-crop.tsx`), and notification emails. Password, email, Google and API tokens open on the web.
- **New projects**: from the Library's +, empty or from a starter, in any of your workspaces.
- **Export and import**: a project, or part of it, as Word, a PDF, Markdown, EPUB, a `.plotra` file and the web's other formats, as of a release, through the share sheet. Word, PDF, RTF and text are set in one of the web's manuscript layouts on Letter or A4; the PDF is the server's print layout (apps/app/lib/io/print-html.ts) turned into pages on the phone with expo-print. Word, Markdown, RTF, text, Plottr and `.plotra` files into a project or as a new one.
- **Plugins**: browse the plugin store and read what a plugin can do. Plugins run on the web; installing, switching and removing them happen there.

## Signing in

The app opens `/mobile/sign-in` on the server in a browser sheet, where you sign in as you do on the web (Google and password managers work there). The page hands a one-time code back on `plotra://sign-in`; the app swaps it, with a verifier only it holds, for an API token at `/api/mobile/token` (see `apps/app/lib/mobile-sign-in.ts` and `packages/auth/src/desktop.ts`). The token can write, lasts a year, is kept in the keychain or keystore, and shows in Settings → API tokens as "Plotra app on <phone>". Signing out revokes it.

## Developing

```sh
bun install            # from the repo root
cd apps/mobile
bunx expo run:ios      # or run:android: a development build (it uses native modules Expo Go may not have)
bun run typecheck && bun run lint && bun run test
```

To point it at a local server, choose "Use your own server" on the first screen and enter your computer's address on the network (for example `http://192.168.1.4:3000`); the phone can't reach `localhost`.

## Push notifications need an EAS project

Expo's push service needs the EAS project id, which `bunx eas-cli init` writes into `app.json` as `extra.eas.projectId`. There isn't one yet, so until there is the app skips registering quietly and Account says notifications aren't available in this version. They also need a development or store build on a real phone (not a simulator). On the server, `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` is optional (only if push security is turned on for the Expo account).

## Releasing

With EAS (`eas.json`): `bunx eas-cli build --profile production --platform all`, then `bunx eas-cli submit`. The bundle id is `ink.plotra.app` on both platforms. `ios/` and `android/` are generated from `app.json`; don't edit them by hand.

## Not on the phone yet

Reactions to passages, comparing a version with now, board versions, installing or switching plugins, a PDF's running header and page numbers, and importing Scrivener, World Anvil or Obsidian. They open on the web from the app. In the story views, these stay on the web: the timeline's zoomable axis, narrative order and dragging, making timelines and calendars, and saving its lane setting (the phone's lane choice is its own); dragging corkboard cards, card sizes and shown fields; the plot grid's thread colours, resolving a thread at a scene, moving threads and fields as columns; making world-bible types and their pages, research notes, fields that link notes or hold several choices, and a shared world from another project. In the maps: the graph's tag filter and shared-world entries, dragging people on the relationship map (it's a circle on the phone), the map of world-bible types (factions, nations), boards' pen drawings, pictures, note cards, arrows, groups and resizing, boards made as maps, and editing dialogues.
